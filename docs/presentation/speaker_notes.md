# ARTEMIS — Speaker Notes
*Authored by Bassam S Faraj Jr., Product Evangelist & Technical Copywriter*

---

## Slide 1 — Title
"Good morning. I’m Bassam S Faraj Jr. Today we’re looking at ARTEMIS — the autonomous AI assistant that lets engineering and QA teams control real Android phones with natural language. Our tagline is simple: Let AI assistants and test suites use real phones like a human."

## Slide 2 — The Problem
"Mobile automation is brittle. Scripts break when a button moves, IDs change, or a toast disappears. Teams spend more time maintaining selectors than validating real user journeys. And most tools can’t reason across apps, recover from unexpected popups, or give IDE-native diagnostics."

## Slide 3 — Product Lineup
"ARTEMIS gives you four ways to work. The Web Console for visual test orchestration, the CLI for CI/CD and benchmark runs, the MCP Server for native IDE control in Antigravity, Claude Code, Windsurf, and Codex, and the Python SDK for embedding into pytest or existing automation frameworks."

## Slide 4 — Core Features
"Under the hood, ARTEMIS combines accessibility hierarchies, OCR, and visual grounding so it can target dynamic UI, custom Canvas, Compose, and Flutter screens. It runs a reactive observe-think-act loop, handles transient controls through click sequences, and shares compressed session history across runs."

## Slide 5 — Benefits
"The outcome is faster QA cycles. Test authors describe intent in plain English instead of XPath. Teams get real-device confidence, IDE-native debugging with logcat and screenshots, and structured Pydantic outputs that plug directly into CI gates."

## Slide 6 — Solutions
"The proof point is AndroidWorld: 99%+ task completion across 20+ apps and 100+ multi-step tasks. That is state-of-the-art. ARTEMIS also installs one-click MCP configs and agent rules, so your AI IDE becomes a senior mobile test engineer out of the box."

## Slide 7 — Evolution Path
"We’re not stopping at Android. The roadmap includes an Android Studio plugin, iOS device support, on-device lightweight VLMs for privacy-first local execution, and real-time duplex voice interaction for hands-free dispatch."

## Slide 8 — Overhead Impact
"ARTEMIS reduces overhead in three ways. Flash runs at 3–5 seconds per step for deterministic tasks, so routine checks are cheap. Pro compresses history instead of truncating it, keeping long-horizon context manageable. And the closed-loop architecture means fewer flaky reruns and less manual selector maintenance."

## Slide 9 — Call to Action
"If your team is ready to move from fragile scripts to autonomous, benchmark-proven mobile testing, start with `./start.sh`, connect your device, and run your first natural-language task in under five minutes."
